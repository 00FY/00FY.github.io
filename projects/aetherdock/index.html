01 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

The problem: resource behavior changes with the workload

A fixed CPU or memory threshold can be too noisy for one container and too late for another. The project focuses on observing each container over time and distinguishing its usual workload behavior from an emerging deviation.

The local and educational setting makes interpretability especially useful: a monitoring signal should help a developer understand a resource trend and decide what to do next.

02 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Runtime metrics and historical context

The project brief describes collecting CPU, memory, network and disk I/O measurements from Docker containers, with historical metrics stored in SQLite. This history supplies context for analyzing time-series behavior rather than examining isolated readings.

Container-specific features and adaptive EWMA baselines provide a way to represent change over time. Isolation Forest contributes an unsupervised view of unusual combinations of resource behavior.

03 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Explainability and user control

The published abstract describes combining per-container baselines, EWMA trend modeling and multivariate Isolation Forest detection without labeled failure data. It emphasizes explanations tied to visible resource trends and decisions reviewed by a user.

That frames anomaly detection as support for investigation and controlled action. The monitoring dashboard and metric history provide the operational context around a flagged event.

04 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Published research and reported findings

The co-authored paper is published in the proceedings of IGNITE 2026 and was added to IEEE Xplore on 30 September 2026. Its complete title is “AetherDock: An Explainable and Predictive Platform for Local Container Monitoring in DevOps Education.”

The abstract reports early-warning behavior in real workloads and controlled stress scenarios, usually 15–30 minutes before the studied anomalies, alongside stable normal-workload behavior and low overhead. This is a paper-reported finding, not a universal lead-time guarantee for arbitrary containers.

SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

The evidence boundary

  • The publication abstract is available; the full published experimental methodology was not accessible during this portfolio update.
  • Reported warning lead times depend on the workloads and stress scenarios studied; they are not a service-level promise.
