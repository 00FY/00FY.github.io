01 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

The problem: an agent’s intent is not authorization

Tool-enabled agents can propose operations that affect external systems or export information. Deciding whether an action is permitted requires business rules, context and accountability in addition to language-model reasoning.

ActionGate explores a control layer between proposal and execution. It is described here as an architecture concept; the policy and approval workflows are not presented as a deployed product.

02 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Risk scoring and deterministic policy

The design includes a Weighted Action Risk Scorer (WARS) concept to assess the consequences of a proposed action. Scoring is paired with explicit policies rather than treated as a substitute for them.

The proposed policy surface includes tool allowlists, outbound restrictions, export-size limits and segregation of duties. The architectural objective is to make an action decision reproducible and inspectable outside the model’s free-text response.

03 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Allow, hold or block

A proposed action passes through risk and policy evaluation before an execution decision. A permitted operation can proceed; an action requiring review is held; an operation outside policy is blocked.

This separation makes a human approval meaningful: approval concerns a specific proposed operation and its context, rather than giving the model unlimited permission for subsequent actions. Detailed execution guarantees remain future implementation work.

04 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Human review and an audit trail

The architecture includes a decision ledger so action proposals, policy decisions and approvals can be examined later. IVR-based authorization using keypad input was explored as one possible review channel.

The central engineering question is how to keep a model flexible while making external side effects governed and attributable. Risk-score calibration, approval binding and enforcement validation still need implementation and testing.

SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

The evidence boundary

  • WARS, the ledger and approval channels are design concepts; no deployment or security certification is claimed.
  • No quantitative risk calibration, bypass resistance or authorization accuracy has been demonstrated in the supplied evidence.
