01 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

The problem: reuse search experience without leaking test information

A pipeline search makes a sequence of preprocessing, feature-selection and classifier decisions. Starting every dataset from scratch discards experience, but transferring previous searches introduces another risk: tuning the retrieval mechanism using information from the tasks it is supposed to evaluate.

TRAJMETA separates training, validation and final tasks. Training tasks create the trajectory bank and shared encoder; validation selects settings; final datasets are used only after those choices are frozen.

02 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Pipeline construction with PPO

The finite search space contains 48 classification pipelines built from three scalers, two feature-selection modes and four classifier families. A PPO agent chooses pipeline stages and receives feedback from inner cross-validation.

The study charges every search method the same 32 pipeline evaluations. Repeated configurations and failed candidates count against the budget, preventing an apparent advantage from free retries or cached evaluations. Outer test labels are kept outside search, retrieval and tuning.

03 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

LSTM representations and trajectory retrieval

A shared LSTM represents ordered search histories. The bank is constructed only from training tasks and uses within-task quality ranks; terminal outcomes are masked from encoder inputs. Dataset metadata first shortlists source tasks, then similarity retrieves relevant trajectories.

Retrieved configurations support an offline warm start. Potential-based reward shaping adds a bounded difference term to PPO, with a zero terminal potential. Retrieval-only, shaped search and cold-start controls make it possible to ask which part of the transfer mechanism actually contributes.

04 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

A controlled study, with an honest outcome

The repository’s study report compares nine methods on three held-out datasets and three seeds: 81 final runs and 2,592 charged evaluations. Iris, German Credit and Sonar form the final test pool. The report retains split provenance, warning records and reproduction instructions.

The 30% improvement hypothesis is not supported. Shaped PPO’s capped target-evaluation statistic is descriptively 9.2% lower than Random Search, while its outer macro-F1 is lower: 0.7923 versus 0.8019. Shaped and retrieval-only aggregate results are equal under this short-budget protocol, so an incremental benefit from shaping was not demonstrated.

05 / PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Engineering discipline around the experiment

The study records exact budgets, dataset checksums, model and bank versions, and cold-start parity. A documented metadata-format amendment preserves prior evidence rather than silently replacing completed results.

Only three final datasets are available, making task-level uncertainty substantial. The report also identifies timeout and final-fit convergence caveats. These limits are part of the research result: reproducibility and clear boundaries matter as much as a favorable point estimate.

SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

The evidence boundary

  • Three final datasets and short search budgets do not establish broad AutoML superiority.
  • No statistically significant transfer gain or 30% improvement is claimed.
  • The report’s timing audit does not establish strict end-to-end wall-time bounds or algorithmic speed comparisons.

Implementation and experiment details are grounded in the repository README and the controlled study report.
